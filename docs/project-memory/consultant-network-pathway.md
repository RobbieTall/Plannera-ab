# Consultant Network and council employed planner pathway

Status: proposed post-MVP product direction. This document records intended eligibility and operating controls; it does not activate matching, credential verification or consultant transmission.

## Fit with the current product

Plannera's project-specific evidence chain identifies professional inputs, prepares a discipline-specific brief, obtains the user's consent, and can submit an exact-scope Expert Review Request to the human-operated referral queue. The existing queue does not automatically match providers, verify credentials, invite bids or promise availability. Its `ASSIGNED` status means an operator actually sent a package. The broader directory, matching and RFQ capability described in `docs/COMMERCIALISATION_WORKFLOWS.md` remains future work. The initial self-reported qualifications disclosure in that document remains the MVP position; any "verified" designation below requires a separate evidence and review process before launch.

## Provider cohorts

The future network can include independent planning consultants, council-employed planners undertaking authorised outside work, surveyors, architects and designers, certifiers, bushfire practitioners, ecologists, arborists, flood and traffic specialists, engineers and other relevant disciplines. Professional categories, reserved titles, licences and insurance requirements need discipline-specific checks before an asserted verification label is shown. Council employment alone is neither a credential nor an endorsement.

The optional council-employed planner pathway is open only where the individual's employer permits the proposed outside work and the individual satisfies their own council's code, policy and approval conditions. Working outside the employing LGA is a default platform exclusion for this cohort, not a safe harbour: adjoining areas, shared services, regional panels, prior matters, relationships or broader employer restrictions may also create conflicts. A planner whose employer does not approve outside work is ineligible.

## Proposed onboarding and controls

1. Collect identity, independent business/contact details, qualifications, experience, service types, service LGAs and availability. Keep employing council, role, approval evidence and restrictions private to authorised operators.
2. Before any work is offered, obtain and review evidence of the employer's written outside-work approval where required by the applicable code or employer policy. Record approved scope, issuing authority, date, expiry/review date and any geographical or subject restrictions. Do not assume an attestation alone establishes permission.
3. Exclude the employing LGA from matching, with configurable wider exclusions. Screen each prospective job against the person's council functions, site, applicant, owner, related entities and known prior/current matters; require the planner to declare actual, potential and perceived conflicts for each offer. Escalate ambiguity to a human review; decline or block work where a conflict cannot be managed.
4. Recheck approval and conflicts when employment, duties, project scope or conditions change and at a defined periodic interval. Suspend eligibility on expiry, withdrawal or unresolved concerns.
5. Keep council systems, time, templates, confidential information, email and insignia separate from private work. Do not suggest preferential access, influence, council endorsement or an assured approval outcome.
6. Show clients an accurate description such as "independent practising town planner" with relevant experience, clearly stating that the work is independent. Do not market employment with a council as an inside track. Give clients a way to raise a conflict concern.

The exact evidence retention period, access permissions, audit trail, dispute process, insurance checks and applicable professional obligations require legal and operational review before launch. Sensitive employment and customer data must not appear in public profiles or analytics.

## Referral flow

Site and proposal evidence → classify required, conditional or recommended input → prepare an exact-scope brief → obtain user consent → screen provider eligibility and conflicts → send to an eligible provider only when an operator or an approved future matching process actually does so → record acceptance or decline → receive the resulting report into the same project with source, date, applicability and conflicts reviewed. A provider may set availability and accept or decline offers; the client can use an outside consultant and upload their report instead. No quote, fee, response time or credential guarantee is implied by a recommendation.

For the current queue, retain its existing consent, minimal contact data, immutable snapshot and truthful status transitions. Do not treat this proposal as authorisation to change APIs, billing, statuses, production flags or the paid A$49/A$749 journey.

## Delivery sequence and acceptance

1. Discovery: validate employer policies with prospective planners, consultant demand, conflicts, client expectations and whether verification is proportionate. Record a legal review of current NSW rules and council-specific policies before public recruitment.
2. Controlled manual pilot: a small number of eligible independent consultants; add council-employed participants only after approval evidence and per-job screening are operational. Operators maintain the evidence and decisions privately.
3. Productise only proven steps: private provider records, eligibility/approval expiry, geographical and matter-based exclusions, per-offer decisions, availability, audit history and client disclosure. Keep matching disabled by default until tested.
4. Acceptance: blocked employing-LGA and wider-restriction cases; expired/withdrawn approval; undisclosed or ambiguous related matter; changed role and proposal; duplicate offers; provider decline; truthful queue transitions; private-data containment; consent withdrawal; and report intake into the exact project scope.

References for policy review: NSW Office of Local Government, [Model Code of Conduct for Local Councils in NSW](https://www.olg.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-02/model-code-of-conduct-for-local-councils-in-nsw.pdf); NSW ICAC, [Outside employment](https://www.icac.nsw.gov.au/prevention/basic-standards/outside-employment) and [Conflicts of interest](https://www.icac.nsw.gov.au/prevention/basic-standards/conflicts-of-interest). Check the council's adopted code, policy and the individual's approval before relying on this pathway.
