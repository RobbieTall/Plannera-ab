Byron Shire Development Control Plan 2014

Chapter D1 Residential Development of the Byron Shire Development Control Plan 2014.

Chapter D1 Residential Development

Chapter D1 contains controls for all forms of residential development including dwelling houses, dual occupancies, multi dwelling housing, secondary dwellings and seniors housing.

Controls address neighbourhood character, amenity, environmental performance and equitable sharing of outlook and solar access.

D1.1 Purpose and application

This chapter applies to all residential development on land zoned for housing purposes unless another chapter provides more specific controls.

Where there is an inconsistency between this chapter and a site specific DCP, the site specific provisions prevail.

D1.1.1 Desired future character

Development must reinforce the coastal village identity of Byron Shire, retaining mature vegetation and responding to topography.

Materials should be lightweight in flood-prone areas to minimise debris risk.

D1.1.2 Consultation

Applicants are encouraged to discuss proposals with adjoining landowners early in the design process to address privacy and overshadowing concerns.

D1.1.3 Ecologically sustainable design

Design dwellings to maximise natural ventilation, include water sensitive urban design and allow 3,000–5,000 litre rainwater tanks for irrigation and toilet flushing.

D1.1.4 Bush fire prone land

On bush fire prone land, design in accordance with Planning for Bush Fire Protection and the relevant BAL construction level.

D1.2 Subdivision design

Subdivision must respond to site constraints and deliver lots capable of accommodating compliant building envelopes and private open space.

D1.2.1 Minimum lot sizes

Observe the LEP minimum lot size. Battle-axe handles must be at least 4 m wide and allow two-way access where serving more than one lot.

D1.2.2 Orientation

Lot layout should allow north-facing living areas and solar access to private open space for at least 3 hours between 9am and 3pm mid-winter.

D1.2.3 Staging

Where larger parcels are subdivided in stages, provide an indicative layout for ultimate development including pedestrian links to surrounding streets.

D1.2.4 Drainage

Lots must have a legal point of discharge. Overland flow paths must be identified and kept clear within drainage easements.

D1.2.5 Corner lots

On corner lots ensure dwellings address both street frontages, with articulation and windows to secondary facades to provide passive surveillance.

D1.3 Streetscape and building form

New development must complement the prevailing streetscape pattern, stepping building mass to follow the natural slope.

D1.3.1 Façade articulation

Provide a change in wall plane at least every 8 metres using bays, balconies or material changes to reduce perceived bulk.

D1.3.2 Roof form

Pitched, skillion or curved roofs are acceptable. Flat parapets must be screened by articulated elements such as blade walls or generous eaves.

D1.3.3 Street entries

Provide a clearly identifiable entry door facing the street with weather protection and natural surveillance of the frontage.

D1.3.4 Adaptable housing

At least 20% of dwellings in multi dwelling housing must be capable of conversion to Livable Housing Design silver level.

D1.3.5 Fences

Front fences in residential zones must be predominantly transparent above 1.2 m and not exceed 1.8 m in height.

D1.3.6 Corner presentation

On corner lots, wrap materials and windows around the corner and avoid blank walls longer than 6 metres to secondary streets.

D1.4 Setbacks

Setbacks ensure adequate separation between buildings, streets and neighbouring properties for privacy, landscaping and view sharing.

D1.4.1 Primary street setbacks

Minimum 6.0 m to the primary street frontage for dwelling houses and dual occupancies. Porches may encroach 1.5 m where the awning remains open-sided.

D1.4.2 Secondary street setbacks

Minimum 3.0 m to secondary frontage. Garages on secondary frontages must be set back a further 1.0 m from the dwelling façade.

D1.4.3 Side setbacks ground floor

1.5 m minimum side setback for single storey walls up to 4.5 m in height.

D1.4.4 Side setbacks upper levels

Upper level walls must be a minimum 3.0 m from side boundaries where containing habitable room windows or balconies.

D1.4.5 Rear setbacks

Provide a minimum 6.0 m rear setback to habitable rooms and private open space at ground level.

D1.4.6 Dual occupancy setbacks

Attached and detached dual occupancies must comply with the primary and secondary street setbacks above and maintain 3.0 m between buildings within the site.

  • Where dwellings are stacked on steep land, increase building separation to 4.5 m for upper levels to protect outlook.
  • Driveways serving two dwellings must be at least 3 m wide with passing bay where length exceeds 30 m.

D1.4.7 Rural residential setbacks

For rural zoned lots, provide 10 m front setback to local roads and 20 m to arterial roads. Outbuildings must be behind the dwelling building line.

D1.4.8 Corner truncation visibility

Maintain a 2 m x 2 m sight triangle at street corners clear of fences, landscaping or letterboxes above 1.0 m height.

D1.5 Site coverage and landscaping

Site coverage limits built form dominance and protects space for landscaping and stormwater infiltration.

D1.5.1 Maximum site coverage

Site coverage must not exceed 50% of the site area for dwelling houses and dual occupancies or 60% for multi dwelling housing.

Eaves, awnings and unroofed decks are excluded from site coverage calculations.

D1.5.2 Deep soil zones

Provide minimum 25% of the site as deep soil with at least one contiguous area of 20 m2 in the front setback and one in the rear.

D1.5.3 Tree planting

Plant two medium canopy trees (8–12 m mature height) within the front setback for dual occupancies and one large tree (>12 m) in rear yards where soil depth permits.

D1.5.4 Water sensitive design

Incorporate rain gardens or infiltration trenches sized at 3% of the connected impervious area to slow stormwater discharge.

D1.5.5 Earthworks

Limit cut or fill to 1.0 m at any point. Use split-level design on sloping sites to follow natural ground and retain existing landform.

D1.6 Building height and envelopes

Building envelopes manage bulk and overshadowing while enabling varied roof forms.

D1.6.1 Height plane

No part of a dwelling may exceed 9.0 m above existing ground level. Lift overruns and chimneys may exceed by up to 1.2 m where centrally located.

D1.6.2 Building envelope for dual occupancy

Dual occupancies must fit within a building envelope projected at 45 degrees from a height of 4.5 m at side and rear boundaries.

D1.6.3 Roof terraces

Roof terraces are permitted only where screening prevents overlooking and overall height remains within the 9.0 m plane.

D1.6.4 Attic accommodation

Habitable attic spaces must be contained within the main roof form with dormer windows set back 1.0 m from side boundaries.

D1.6.5 Steep land

On slopes greater than 15%, step buildings with the slope and avoid large undercroft areas to reduce visual bulk.

D1.7 Solar access and energy efficiency

Dwellings must achieve good solar access to living rooms and private open space while limiting overshadowing of neighbours.

D1.7.1 Solar access to PPOS

Provide at least 3 hours of direct sunlight to 50% of the principal private open space between 9am and 3pm mid-winter for each dwelling.

D1.7.2 Overshadowing of neighbours

Development must not reduce sunlight to the north-facing living room windows of adjoining dwellings to less than 3 hours between 9am and 3pm mid-winter.

D1.7.3 Natural ventilation

Habitable rooms should be dual aspect or have openable windows totalling at least 10% of floor area to promote cross ventilation.

D1.7.4 Shading devices

Provide operable shading to west-facing glazing and awnings to north windows sized to exclude summer sun while admitting winter sun.

D1.7.5 Photovoltaics

Provide roof space clear of shadowing for installation of 3 kW solar PV systems on each dwelling where feasible.

D1.8 Privacy and amenity

Design must prevent direct overlooking between habitable rooms and private open space of neighbouring properties.

D1.8.1 Window placement

Offset upper level windows or raise sill heights to 1.6 m above finished floor level where they face side or rear boundaries within 3 m.

D1.8.2 Screening to balconies

Provide privacy screens to side edges of balconies within 3 m of a boundary, with a minimum height of 1.5 m and maximum 25% openings.

D1.8.3 Acoustic separation

Locate noisy plant rooms, driveways and communal areas away from bedroom windows. Provide 1.8 m high acoustic fencing where necessary.

D1.8.4 Lighting

External lighting must be baffled to prevent light spill onto adjoining properties while maintaining safe entry paths.

D1.8.5 Overlooking from common circulation

Screen elevated walkways and stairs to a height of 1.5 m where they overlook neighbouring private spaces.

D1.9 Private open space

Each dwelling must include usable private open space (POS) with good solar access and direct connection to living areas.

D1.9.1 Ground level POS

Provide a principal private open space (PPOS) of at least 50 m2 with a minimum dimension of 4 m for dwelling houses and dual occupancies.

D1.9.2 Upper level balconies

Where ground level POS is not feasible, provide balconies of at least 12 m2 with a minimum dimension of 2.5 m for upper level dwellings.

D1.9.3 Sunlight to POS

Design buildings so that at least 50% of the PPOS receives 3 hours direct sunlight mid-winter; communal open space may supplement where individual POS is shaded.

D1.9.4 Common open space

Multi dwelling developments of 20 or more dwellings must provide common open space equal to 10% of the site area with seating and shade trees.

D1.9.5 Clothes drying

Provide screened clothes drying areas with solar access or condensate dryers to avoid reliance on balcony rails.

D1.10 Car parking and access

Parking and access for residential development must comply with Chapter B4. Parking should be located behind the building line and minimise visual dominance of garages.

D1.10.1 Garage design

Garage widths must not exceed 50% of the dwelling façade. Where two garages face the street, provide articulation and landscaping between doors.

D1.10.2 Basement parking

Basement car parks must project no more than 1.0 m above natural ground and be screened by landscaping. Ventilation grilles are to be integrated into façades.

D1.10.3 Visitor parking

Visitor spaces must be clearly signposted and accessible without security gates. Share visitor bays with loading areas only where no conflict occurs.

D1.10.4 Driveway gradients

Residential driveways must not exceed 20% grade and must include 2 m transition zones at the property boundary.

D1.10.5 Pedestrian safety

Provide sight splays at driveway exits and install convex mirrors where walls limit visibility. Separate pedestrian entries from vehicular crossings where possible.

D1.11 Waste and recycling

Waste management facilities must be sized for Council collection services and located to minimise impacts on amenity.

D1.11.1 Bin storage

Provide screened bin storage areas sized for Council bins for each dwelling. For dual occupancies, provide an on-site bin presentation area clear of driveways.

D1.11.2 Collection vehicles

Where on-site collection is proposed, demonstrate turning paths for waste vehicles and ensure trucks can enter and leave in a forward direction.

D1.11.3 Chutes and rooms

Buildings of three storeys or more must provide garbage chutes with interim storage rooms on each level and a ventilated ground floor bin room.

D1.11.4 Bulky waste

Provide a secure bulky waste storage area of at least 6 m2 for developments of 10 or more dwellings.

D1.12 Water and stormwater

Design must mitigate downstream impacts by retaining and treating stormwater close to source.

D1.12.1 On-site detention

Provide on-site detention sized to limit post development peak discharge to pre-development 1 in 5 year storm levels with allowable release rate agreed by Council.

D1.12.2 Rainwater tanks

Install rainwater tanks with a minimum capacity of 5,000 litres for dwellings larger than 120 m2 GFA, connected to toilets and laundry.

D1.12.3 Overland flow

Identify and sign major overland flow paths. Fences crossing flow paths must include openings or be post-and-rail construction.

D1.12.4 Erosion control

Implement erosion and sediment controls consistent with the Blue Book for all stages of subdivision and building works.

D1.13 Flooding and coastal hazard

Development in flood prone or coastal hazard areas must protect life and property and maintain evacuation routes.

D1.13.1 Flood planning level

Set habitable floor levels at or above the flood planning level plus 500 mm freeboard. Car parks and storerooms below FPL must use flood compatible materials.

D1.13.2 Emergency access

Provide at least one flood free access route to a public road at or above the flood planning level. Identify refuge areas on upper floors where necessary.

D1.13.3 Coastal setback

Where land is subject to coastal erosion, comply with coastal hazard lines and incorporate relocatable building design where required by the LEP.

D1.13.4 Evacuation signage

Multi dwelling developments must display flood evacuation signage in common areas showing exit routes and assembly points.

D1.14 Noise and air quality

Sensitive development near roads, rail or industry must incorporate acoustic and air quality mitigation.

D1.14.1 Acoustic reports

Where development is within 60 m of an arterial road, submit an acoustic report demonstrating compliance with internal noise criteria in accordance with the NSW Noise Policy for Industry.

D1.14.2 Mechanical plant

Locate air-conditioning compressors and pool pumps away from neighbouring bedrooms and provide acoustic enclosures where necessary.

D1.14.3 Ventilation intakes

Position natural ventilation openings and mechanical intakes away from vehicle exhaust points and loading bays.

D1.15 Accessibility and inclusion

Residential development should accommodate people of all ages and abilities.

D1.15.1 Universal access

Provide at least one continuous accessible path from the street or parking area to the primary entrance of each dwelling at grade or via ramp.

D1.15.2 Lift provision

Multi dwelling buildings of three storeys or more must include a lift servicing all levels including basement parking.

D1.15.3 Adaptable bathrooms

At least one bathroom in each dwelling must allow future installation of grab rails with reinforced walls and circulation space of 1.5 m diameter.

D1.15.4 Letterboxes and storage

Provide letterboxes and parcel lockers at an accessible height with weather protection. Secure storage cages of at least 6 m3 are required for each apartment.

D1.16 Specific housing types

The following provisions apply in addition to the general controls above.

D1.16.1 Secondary dwellings

Secondary dwellings are limited to 60 m2 GFA unless otherwise permitted by the LEP. Provide one on-site parking space which may be in tandem with the primary dwelling.

D1.16.2 Dual occupancies

Dual occupancies must share a common driveway where practicable and provide private open space areas that are directly accessible from living rooms.

D1.16.3 Multi dwelling housing

Arrange dwellings around communal open space and provide pedestrian entries from the street for ground floor dwellings to activate the frontage.

D1.16.4 Seniors housing

Comply with SEPP Housing for Seniors or People with a Disability. Provide mobility scooter parking and charging near entries.

D1.16.5 Manor houses and terraces

Manor houses must present as a large dwelling to the street with a single shared driveway crossover and individual entries for each dwelling where possible.

D1.17 Infrastructure and services

Development must be serviced by appropriate infrastructure without adverse impacts on adjoining land.

D1.17.1 Utilities

Demonstrate that water, sewer, electricity and telecommunications can be provided to each lot. Locate substations clear of the primary streetscape or integrate within building form.

D1.17.2 Firefighting water

Provide hydrants and fire appliance access in accordance with the NCC and local water authority requirements.

D1.17.3 Easements

Do not build structures within easements unless approved by the benefiting authority. Landscaping within easements must allow maintenance access.

D1.18 Coastal and scenic protection

Protect coastal character and scenic values through sensitive siting and materials.

D1.18.1 View sharing

On ridgelines and coastal escarpments, modulate building form to retain existing view corridors between dwellings.

D1.18.2 Colours and materials

Use recessive colours and non-reflective materials on roofs and walls in visually prominent locations.

D1.18.3 Lighting spill

Minimise light spill into adjoining bushland and beaches by using low level, downward facing luminaires.

D1.19 Crime prevention

Design should enhance safety and surveillance consistent with CPTED principles.

D1.19.1 Passive surveillance

Provide windows overlooking streets, lanes and communal areas. Avoid high solid fences that remove casual surveillance.

D1.19.2 Entry security

Provide lighting to entries and pathways controlled by motion sensors. Use semi-transparent fencing to maintain visibility.

D1.19.3 Mail and parcel safety

Locate mailboxes in a visible, well-lit location near the main pedestrian entry to deter theft.

D1.20 Heritage and character areas

Development in heritage conservation areas must respect historic scale, form and detailing.

D1.20.1 Building form

In Bangalow and Mullumbimby conservation areas, adopt roof pitches, verandah elements and materials consistent with contributory dwellings in the street.

D1.20.2 Additions to heritage items

Locate additions to the rear of heritage items and distinguish them from original fabric while being sympathetic in scale.

D1.20.3 Car parking for heritage items

Avoid new driveways that remove original street plantings. Utilise existing rear lanes for parking where available.
