Byron Shire Development Control Plan 2014

This consolidated version of the Byron Shire Development Control Plan 2014 provides guidance on development within the Byron local government area. It should be read alongside the Byron Local Environmental Plan and other state planning instruments.

Chapter B4 Traffic, Parking and Access

Objective: To ensure that vehicle access, parking supply and internal circulation are safe, legible and reflect the needs of the proposed land use. Parking design must minimise conflicts between vehicles, pedestrians and cyclists.

Car parking rates: Development must provide on-site parking consistent with the following rates unless a site specific traffic assessment demonstrates an alternative solution with equal or better outcomes:

  • Dual occupancy (detached or attached): Minimum of two (2) resident spaces, plus one (1) visitor space per dwelling. Visitor spaces may be provided in a shared parking court if access remains clear for emergency vehicles.
  • Multi dwelling housing: 1.5 resident spaces per dwelling plus one (1) visitor space per four dwellings.
  • Secondary dwellings: One (1) space, which may be tandem with the principal dwelling where practical.

Parking design: Parking and access must comply with AS 2890 for driveway gradients, aisle widths and bay dimensions. Driveway crossovers should be consolidated to reduce impacts on street trees and on-street parking supply. Where two driveways are proposed, they must be separated by at least one metre of landscaped verge.

Chapter D1 Residential Development

Building envelopes: New dwellings and dual occupancies must demonstrate a building envelope that fits within a maximum height plane of 9 metres above natural ground level, with any rooftop structures contained within this plane.

Setbacks: Street setbacks should align with prevailing front setbacks. Where no predominant setback exists, a minimum of 6 metres to the primary street frontage and 3 metres to the secondary frontage applies. Garages facing the street must be set back at least one metre behind the main building line. Side and rear setbacks are a minimum of 1.5 metres for single storey elements and 3 metres for two storey elements, increasing where windows directly overlook habitable rooms or private open space of adjoining dwellings.

Private open space: Provide a principal private open space area of at least 50 square metres with a minimum dimension of 4 metres, directly accessible from the main living area. For upper-level dwellings, balconies should provide at least 12 square metres with a minimum dimension of 2.5 metres.

Chapter B9 Landscaping

Landscape objectives: Landscaping must retain significant vegetation, reinforce neighbourhood character and provide deep soil zones capable of supporting canopy trees. Development is encouraged to use locally endemic species to improve biodiversity and manage urban heat.

Deep soil: Provide deep soil zones equal to at least 20% of the site area, with a minimum dimension of 3 metres. At least one deep soil area should be located within the front setback to support medium canopy trees and to soften built form when viewed from the street.

Tree planting: For dual occupancies, plant a minimum of two medium canopy trees (mature height 8–12 metres) in the front setback and one medium or large tree in the rear setback. Tree protection measures must be shown on plans where works occur within the tree protection zone of existing vegetation.

Chapter C3 Flood Risk Management

Flood planning levels: Floor levels for habitable rooms must be set at or above the flood planning level as defined in the relevant Council flood study or floodplain risk management plan. Utilities and storage areas below this level must use flood-compatible materials and allow for rapid clean-up after inundation.

Evacuation and access: Development must demonstrate safe evacuation during a flood event, including provision of at least one flood free pedestrian access route and consideration of emergency vehicle access where practical.

This summary is provided for planning guidance. Applicants should consult the full Byron Shire Development Control Plan 2014 and seek professional advice for site-specific proposals.
